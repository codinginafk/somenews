**Short answer:** While the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya have seen incredible athletic achievements, including China's dominance in the medal tally, severe administrative errors—ranging from playing the wrong national anthem to housing athletes on cargo ships—have sparked widespread criticism of the host's management.

## 1. What the medal tally shows

The competition on the field has been fiercely contested. According to the official medal counts, China maintains its supremacy at the top of the list with a staggering 338 medals (167 gold, 89 silver, and 82 bronze). India has also delivered a strong performance, securing the 4th position with a total of 85 medals (though as we explored in our guide on [how to read rankings](/how-to-read-rankings/), such tallies don't always tell the full story), 21 of which are gold, highlighted by a historic victory from the Indian women's hockey team.

## 2. The administrative errors and controversies

Despite the athletic success, three major controversies have overshadowed the games:

**North Korea National Anthem:** On the first day of men's field hockey, the national anthem of North Korea (*Aegukga*) was mistakenly played for the South Korean team prior to its match against Bangladesh. This caused immediate confusion on the ground, leading officials to issue a swift apology.

**Long Jump Measurement Controversy:** Indian long jumper Shaili Singh missed out on securing a medal after her jump was measured from the incorrect rear pit mark. Officials cited a lack of side-angle cameras as the reason for this oversight. In response, Singh has officially appealed the decision to the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS).

**Athletes Housed on a Container Ship:** Overcrowding and management delays forced roughly 15,000 athletes to be housed in temporary wooden container houses and aboard the *Costa Serena* cruise ship. This lack of proper accommodation has raised serious questions about the host's preparedness.

## 3. What it means for the host nation

As a result of missing camera angles in crucial track and field events, geopolitical gaffes during national anthems, and deeply flawed accommodation strategies, international sports committees are reportedly handing Japan a failing grade for its hosting efforts.
