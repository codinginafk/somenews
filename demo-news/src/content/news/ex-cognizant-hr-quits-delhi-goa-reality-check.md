**Short answer:** Arpita Sahu, an HR professional with an MBA and four and a half years of corporate experience across Info Edge India Ltd and Cognizant, left Delhi for Goa in October 2026 to seek purpose. Her public video logs confirm the relocation, but dismantle the fantasy of carefree coastal living. Without a steady paycheck, she reported acute financial dread, broken focus, and the sudden disorientation of waking up on Monday morning without an office structure.

## 1. What the viral videos document

In videos published to Instagram in mid-October 2026, Arpita Sahu documented her departure from Delhi and arrival in Goa. After completing her MBA and spending four and a half years in corporate human resources, she chose to resign from her structured position.

"I packed all my life in these suitcases... I left Delhi and I reached Goa I left my corporate job," Sahu stated while filming her luggage. "I worked hard for four and a half years, finished my MBA and then now I'm moving to Goa."

Her LinkedIn profile verifies professional service at Info Edge India Ltd and Cognizant. Stepping onto the platform at a Goa railway station, she addressed the gulf between taking a holiday and rebuilding an adult life from scratch:

"I reached station and it was such a different feeling. I understand that Goa is a place that people come to take a trip, travel with their friends and family. But for me, I'm moving here, and, you know, every time I move to a new city, it's the same question: whether I'll find my people, whether I'll find my purpose. I really hope that, you know, this place becomes a place which I can call home. I find my people here. Until that happens, I'm going to keep searching. I'm going to keep looking in every corner of the world."

She encouraged viewers seeking meaning not to give up, noting that setbacks can help locate the right place or community.

## 2. The first Monday without an office

The psychological cost of voluntary unemployment surfaced on her first Monday without work. In traditional employment, Monday establishes the weekly schedule. For Sahu, the absence of an office timetable produced unease rather than pure relaxation.

"There's an unsaid pressure that I have to do well, and there's a certain amount of money in my bank account which I know will not last for long," Sahu acknowledged. "My attention span has gone for a toss. I'm not able to watch anything without picking up my phone and looking at Instagram, scrolling and looking at the number of followers."

A salaried job delivers income, daily predictability, defined targets, and measurable momentum. Removing that framework forces a person to manufacture an artificial structure from scratch, as seen in wider studies on [workplace burnout realities](/bank-strike-human-cost-sunday-duty/). Instead of deep relaxation, Sahu found her attention captured by digital metrics, echoing patterns analyzed in our reports on [algorithmic attention loops](/brazil-bricklayer-ai-girlfriend-tiktok-fact-check/).

## 3. The debate: Coastal retreat vs economic reality

Public response to Sahu's videos highlighted contrasting approaches to career breaks. Several commenters pointed out that Goa's tourist-driven, relaxed pace offers few professional safety nets. "Leave Goa its a slow life you need a faster state to be stable. How about mumbai," one commenter argued, urging a return to commercial hubs.

Other viewers took a philosophical perspective. "Life has no purpose to be achieved. Death will come anyway—so why not live fully while you are alive? Don't live for tomorrow. Be in the present, be aware, be a witness," another user wrote.

The tension mirrors broader debates on whether workers should prioritize financial reserves, career mobility, or personal freedom. While social media celebrates dramatic resignations, real-world data demonstrates that living costs quickly erode personal savings without recurring cash flow. Much like international [happiness rankings and lived conditions](/finland-happiest-bangladesh-reality/), relocating across state borders changes the physical scenery, but does not instantly resolve underlying questions of community, vocation, and financial security.
