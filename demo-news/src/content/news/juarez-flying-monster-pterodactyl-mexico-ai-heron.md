**Short answer:** The creature filmed over Ciudad Juárez is not a pterodactyl. Mexican authorities say it is most likely a great blue heron, a bird with a wingspan reaching 6.5 feet. A palaeontologist identified multiple anatomical errors in the footage, and a prominent ufologist admitted that parts of the viral compilation were enhanced with artificial intelligence. Pterosaurs went extinct approximately 66 million years ago, and no scientific evidence supports their survival to the present day. Pterodactyl are said to have lived near the ocean and this sighting was no where near the ocean and no prehistoric fossils of pterodactyl had been found in the area or around it.

## 1. What the viral clips show

Multiple videos began circulating on TikTok and X in November 2026, reportedly filmed in and around Ciudad Juárez in Mexico's Chihuahua state. The footage shows a large, dark-winged silhouette flying through the sky.

One daytime clip shows the shape soaring over rooftops. A separate nighttime recording, said to come from a home security camera in the Felipe Ángeles neighbourhood, appears to show a similar creature. A third account, broadcast on Mexican UFO researcher Jaime Maussan's programme Interestelar, describes a child named Fernando Arteaga witnessing the creature attack a dog and attempt to lift the animal before releasing it and flying away.

X user Chris Costin shared one video, tagging Elon Musk's AI chatbot Grok and asking whether it was a pterodactyl. Grok dismissed the possibility, responding that the silhouette was most likely a large bird filmed from below against the sky and that pterosaurs went extinct roughly 66 million years ago.

The footage rapidly gained millions of views, with comments comparing it to scenes from the Jurassic Park film franchise.

## 2. Why experts say the footage is fake

Palaeontologist Evan Jevnikar addressed the videos in an Instagram post, stating plainly that the creature shown is not real, for several reasons:

| Problem | What Jevnikar said | Why it matters |
| :- | :- | :- |
| **Wing movement** | A pterosaur that large would not constantly flap its wings | Large flying animals glide. Hawks, vultures, and albatrosses spend most of their flight soaring on thermals, not flapping continuously. |
| **Skin texture** | The creature appears smooth and scaly | Fossil evidence shows pterosaurs had pycnofibres, a feather-like covering, not bare reptilian skin. |
| **Species match** | The shape resembles a Pteranodon | Pteranodon fossils are associated with ancient marine environments in central North America, not the desert-adjacent terrain of Chihuahua state. |
| **Overall assessment** | "Putting all this together, it just looks fake to me" | Multiple anatomical inconsistencies align with a digital fabrication rather than a living animal. |

VICE separately noted that one of the creatures in the compilation more closely resembled a frigatebird than any prehistoric reptile.

## 3. The AI admission and official response

The most significant detail in this story is not the bird identification. It is the acknowledgment that artificial intelligence was used to alter at least some of the footage.

Jaime Maussan, the Mexican UFO researcher who broadcast the clips and speculated that the creature may have come from another dimension, is a prominent figure in ufology circles. His involvement lent the videos an air of authority for viewers already inclined to believe. However, reporting from multiple outlets confirmed that parts of the viral compilation were digitally enhanced using AI tools, a detail that undermines the entire body of footage.

Ciudad Juárez Civil Protection director Sergio Rodríguez issued an official statement dismissing the claims as unfounded. Rodríguez said the videos cannot be considered evidence of a real event and characterized them as digital entertainment content. He urged the public not to spread misinformation.

TV Azteca reported that unnamed experts assessed the silhouette as a great blue heron, a wading bird native to the Americas with a wingspan that can reach 6.5 feet (roughly 2 metres). When filmed from below against a bright sky, a heron in flight can produce a silhouette that looks far larger and more unusual than the bird actually is.

## 4. The pattern of AI-enhanced viral claims

This incident fits a growing pattern where AI image and video tools lower the bar for manufacturing convincing visual evidence. Similar dynamics played out in the [viral Brazilian AI girlfriend TikTok](/brazil-bricklayer-ai-girlfriend-tiktok-fact-check/) case, where AI-generated content drove millions of views before the fabrication was identified. The [Kim Jong Un death rumours](/kim-jong-un-death-rumours-brain-haemorrhage-fact-check/) followed an identical cycle of viral claim, mass engagement, and eventual debunking.

The Juárez footage combined something real (a large bird filmed at a distance) with something manufactured (AI-enhanced clips designed to make the shape look prehistoric). That combination is more persuasive than pure fabrication, because viewers who see the genuine bird footage first are primed to accept the enhanced versions as authentic.

Pterosaurs, including the Pteranodon that Maussan specifically named, went extinct in the mass extinction event approximately 66 million years ago. They flew using wings made of a skin membrane supported by an elongated finger bone, unlike modern birds. There is no scientific evidence that any pterosaur species survived to the present day.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Could any pterosaur species have survived extinction?**
A: No. Pterosaurs disappeared in the Cretaceous-Paleogene mass extinction roughly 66 million years ago. The fossil record shows no lineage surviving past that boundary, and no credible scientific study has ever documented a living pterosaur.

**Q: How big is a great blue heron, and could it be mistaken for something prehistoric?**
A: A great blue heron stands up to 4.5 feet tall with a wingspan of about 6.5 feet. When filmed from below against a bright sky, its silhouette can appear disproportionately large and its slow wingbeats can resemble popular depictions of pterosaurs.

**Q: Did Jaime Maussan admit the footage was AI-enhanced?**
A: Maussan broadcast the footage and speculated about interdimensional origins. Reporting from multiple outlets confirmed that parts of the viral compilation were enhanced with AI, though Maussan's programme did not clearly distinguish between unedited and enhanced clips.
