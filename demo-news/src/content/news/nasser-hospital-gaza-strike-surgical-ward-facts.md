**Short answer:** Gaza Civil Defence crews and hospital officials confirm that an early Thursday Israeli warplane missile hit the second floor of a building at Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis, damaging surgical wards and intensive care units. At least five Palestinians were killed, including a surgeon, and around 20 others were injured. Claims circulating online that a senior Hamas security figure was hiding inside remain unverified, Israel's military gave no immediate public comment, and independent reporters remain barred from checking the site directly.

## 1. What happened inside the surgical wing

The strike hit Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis, the main referral hospital still serving southern Gaza. According to Gaza's Hamas-run Civil Defence agency and witnesses at the compound, a single missile fired from a warplane tore directly into the second floor of a ward building early on Thursday morning.

That level houses both men's and women's surgical wards alongside the hospital's intensive care units. Video and field reports shared by medical workers showed blown-out medical equipment, broken ceiling panels, and heavy rubble across treatment rooms.

Key confirmed casualty and rescue details from the scene include:

| Detail | Reported figure or status | Reporting body |
| :- | :- | :- |
| **Confirmed deaths** | At least 5 killed (initial AFP count of 6) | Gaza Civil Defence / medics |
| **Medical staff hit** | At least 1 surgeon killed (Dr. Rezik Abushira) | Local hospital officials |
| **Injured** | Around 20 patients and staff wounded | Nasser Medical Complex |
| **Target wing** | Second floor housing ICU and surgical rooms | Hospital director and eyewitnesses |
| **Recovery aid** | Joint recovery under ICRC support | Civil Defence / Red Cross |

Civil Defence spokesman Mahmoud Basal initially told Agence France-Presse that six people had died before medics settled on a confirmed count of at least five fatalities while clearing concrete debris. Teams from the International Committee of the Red Cross helped local responders extract victims from damaged rooms.

## 2. Competing claims over why the building was hit

Soon after images of the strike spread across messaging apps, local activists began posting claims that the Israeli missile may have aimed at a senior Hamas security figure taking shelter inside the medical compound. Those posts quickly turned a hospital casualty report into an online argument over military targets.

At this stage, readers need to separate what is documented from what is still unproven:

- **What Israel usually argues:** The Israeli military has repeatedly accused Hamas fighters of operating inside or underneath Gaza hospitals.
- **What Hamas says:** Hamas denies using medical centers as bases or shields for armed commanders.
- **What the IDF has said here:** At the time initial reports published, the Israel Defense Forces had offered no public comment explaining why the Khan Younis ward building was struck.
- **What journalists can verify:** International news organizations, including the BBC, explicitly note that Israeli bans on independent foreign media access inside Gaza prevent outside reporters from inspecting bombed rooms or checking activist rumors firsthand.

Until military logs or independent forensic teams inspect the second floor, no third party can confirm whether a militant target was present or whether the strike hit civilians and medical personnel alone.

## 3. Why Nasser Hospital carries such high stakes

Damage to Nasser Medical Complex hits southern Gaza harder than most buildings because few functioning operating theaters remain open across the territory.

For displaced families crowded around Khan Younis, Nasser Hospital is not an ordinary clinic. Heavy winter rains recently flooded makeshift tent camps holding tens of thousands of displaced people, increasing cases of infection, trauma, and emergency childbirth. Losing intensive care beds and surgical suites cuts off care that smaller field clinics simply cannot handle.

This is also not the first time the compound has come under fire. Back in August 2025, a double Israeli strike hit Nasser Hospital and killed more than 20 people, a toll that included health workers and working journalists. Each repeat incident worsens staff shortages when surviving doctors and nurses hesitate to stay near multi-story wards.

Wider Middle East security incidents have kept civilian infrastructure exposed across neighboring regions as well, as seen in recent [drone and missile strikes hitting civilian airports in Saudi Arabia](/saudi-airport-attack-indian-casualties-fact-check/) and [missile threats aimed at bridges and energy grids in Ukraine](/zelensky-warns-russia-strike-dnipro-bridges-infrastructure/). Across conflict zones, hospitals and transit hubs increasingly find themselves on the front line.

## 4. What the October 2025 ceasefire toll looks like

Social media posts often treat the war in Gaza as paused whenever a ceasefire announcement holds on paper. Data tracked on the ground tells a much harsher story.

According to figures maintained by Gaza's Hamas-run health ministry, more than 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the October 2025 ceasefire arrangement came into effect. United Nations agencies rely on those health ministry tallies during conflict tracking and have long treated them as broadly reliable indicators of documented hospital fatalities.

Meanwhile, total cumulative casualties continue climbing:

- **Total war toll since October 2023:** Over 74,645 people killed across Gaza, according to local health records.
- **Original trigger of the war:** The Hamas-led assault on southern Israel on October 7, 2023, which killed about 1,200 people and resulted in 251 hostages being taken into Gaza.
- **Truce friction:** Both Israel and Hamas accuse the other side of repeatedly breaking ceasefire rules through cross-border fire and delayed commitments.

Even while broader Middle East tensions continue rattling shipping lanes and driving [surprising shifts in global oil supplies](/global-oil-market-surplus-glut-iran-war-data/), humanitarian agencies warn that sporadic strikes inside truce windows still produce mass-casualty hospital events.

## 5. How medical protection works under international law

Under international humanitarian law, hospitals receive special protected status during armed conflicts. Attacking a functioning medical ward is normally unlawful unless the building is actively used to commit acts harmful to the enemy outside its humanitarian function, and even then an attacker must issue prior warnings and follow strict proportionality rules.

In practice, proving or disproving whether a room had been misused requires transparent after-action evidence. Without clear video, recovered weapons, or independent medical-legal inspection on the ground, competing narratives harden immediately along political lines.

For families outside Nasser Hospital on Thursday morning, legal debates offered little comfort while civil defence crews cleared masonry from surgical beds and tried to move surviving intensive care patients to safer rooms.
