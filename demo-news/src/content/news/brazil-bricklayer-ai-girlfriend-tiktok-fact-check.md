**Short answer:** The woman was entirely fictional. Edimar Rocha Martins, a 49-year-old bricklayer from Maranhão, sold his belongings and traveled 1,931 km by bus to Belo Horizonte after watching videos by "Jéssica Oliveira," a profile with 300,000 followers promising romance and financial support. Broadcasters and municipal workers confirmed the account used synthetic imagery to harvest engagement for online sales.

## 1. What the viral account promised

The social media profile operated under the names "Jéssica Oliveira" and "Jéssica Fazendeira" across TikTok, Facebook, and Kwai. Videos displayed an attractive woman living on a countryside estate in Minas Gerais, speaking directly to working-class men. In one widely viewed clip, the avatar stated: "I really want to support you, I want you to come to my farm and never work again in your life."

For Martins, who was coping with severe grief and isolation following the death of his mother, the invitation appeared genuine. He followed the account closely and interpreted the broadcast videos as a personal offer of partnership. On October 9, 2026, Martins liquidated his tools and household goods to buy a long-distance bus ticket. He spent roughly 36 hours on the road, covering 1,931 km from Imperatriz to Belo Horizonte in pursuit of a new life.

## 2. What municipal records and broadcasters found

Martins arrived in Belo Horizonte on October 11 with printed photographs of the account avatar. He walked the streets asking pedestrians and shopkeepers for directions to her ranch, but nobody recognized the face. Running out of money, he visited regional radio broadcaster Rádio Itatiaia, where reporters informed him that the profile was an artificial persona generated by machine learning tools.

"It only brings sadness, discouragement, and depression," Martins told reporters outside the studio. "Is there anything worse than loneliness? I didn't find her, but I'll find another one, and it will be in the same place I went, and I'll go again."

With his savings exhausted, Martins approached Belo Horizonte municipal migrant assistance. Social workers provided him with hot meals, hygiene facilities, and an inter-state bus ticket to Augustinópolis in Tocantins, allowing him to stay with relatives. Local authorities noted that Martins is the third man in under six months to travel to Belo Horizonte after pursuing fabricated online personas.

## 3. The commercial model behind synthetic engagement

Following regional coverage, the anonymous operator of the TikTok profile posted a video conceding that the avatar was generated with artificial intelligence. The creator denied ever messaging Martins directly, stating: "I came here to state the obvious: I am made of artificial intelligence. I have never spoken to anyone with this profile. The intention was to gain engagement for sale." The invitation clip was subsequently removed.

The incident highlights a growing shift from simple spam toward automated parasocial exploitation. Similar dynamics surrounding platform incentives and algorithmic manipulation have appeared across [recent streaming controversies](/canadian-streamer-john-ly-vietnam-slap-racist-incident/) and corporate [AI companion rollouts](/instagram-muse-ai-assistant-privacy-debate/). Synthetic channels deploy computer-generated characters like [experimental virtual models](/local-llm-vtuber-test/) to build massive followings, monetizing follower attention through affiliate product marketing.

The human damage of unchecked synthetic relationships extends well beyond viral scams. In the United States, companion chatbot developer Character.AI faced multiple wrongful death lawsuits after the 2024 suicide of 14-year-old Sewell Setzer, who developed severe emotional dependence on a bot. Character.AI filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection after reporting operating losses of $8 million to $9 million each month, prompting a $20 million intellectual property licensing bid from Disney. As synthetic personas proliferate across commercial feeds, the boundary between automated marketing and emotional deception continues to erode.
