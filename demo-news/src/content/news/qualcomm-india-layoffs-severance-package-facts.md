**Short answer:** Qualcomm has started a workforce reduction in India expected to affect around 1,000 employees, or roughly 5% to 6% of its local headcount. Reports from The Economic Times state affected workers are offered three months of salary, tenure payouts, and one extra month of pay if they sign exit papers within three days. However, Qualcomm has not publicly confirmed those severance terms, and the exit of senior, long-serving engineers shows this move goes beyond simple low-performance firings.

## 1. Breaking down the reported severance offer

Much of the discussion among Indian chip engineers centers on how quickly workers must decide whether to accept separation documents. Multiple workforce reports outline a three-part formula for departing staff in India:

| Package Component | Reported Term | Confirmation Status |
| :- | :- | :- |
| **Base severance** | 3 months of salary | Unconfirmed by Qualcomm |
| **Tenure bonus** | Extra pay scaled by years of service | Unconfirmed by Qualcomm |
| **Quick-sign incentive** | 1 extra month salary if signed within 3 days | Unconfirmed by Qualcomm |
| **Contract variations** | Final amounts vary by employment contract | Depends on individual offer |

The three-day deadline draws the sharpest attention. In corporate exits, offering an extra month of pay for quick signatures encourages employees to finalize mutual separation paperwork promptly and waive future disputes rather than drag out notice-period negotiations.

Still, one point deserves repeating: Qualcomm has made no official public statement confirming every line of this payout structure. Affected staff must check their personal separation letters and local contract rules rather than assuming a uniform payout applies to every band.

## 2. Where the India cuts are landing

India serves as one of Qualcomm's most important global engineering bases outside the United States. The cuts began quietly in Bengaluru and are projected to extend across all four of Qualcomm's main Indian hubs:

- **Bengaluru:** Initial notices reportedly hit engineering teams here first, catching many long-tenured specialists off guard.
- **Hyderabad:** Major modem, chip design, and software integration groups sit in the city's hardware corridor.
- **Delhi-NCR and Chennai:** Research, wireless testing, and platform support offices are also expected to see reductions.

Taken together, losing roughly 1,000 roles trims 5% to 6% of Qualcomm's India workforce. More telling than the raw headcount is who is leaving. Reports highlight cuts across wireless radio frequency (RF), Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and global navigation satellite system (GNSS) teams, including veteran engineers who spent years building core mobile connectivity features.

## 3. Performance review vs. wider restructuring

When Qualcomm chief executive Cristiano Amon announced the global restructuring plan on October 20, 2026, right after the company's annual performance cycle, leadership framed it around accountability and aligning teams with shifting business goals.

Yet two pieces of financial and operational evidence show deeper restructuring at play:

1. **Senior engineers are on the list:** Pure performance purges usually trim recent hires or staff placed on formal coaching plans. Dropping seasoned RF and Wi-Fi architects suggests whole project layers and mature product maintenance teams are being shrunk or merged.
2. **US business leaders shaped the list:** Reporting indicates managers looked beyond individual annual ratings to include team-level reviews and priorities set directly by senior business unit leaders in the United States.
3. **Massive restructuring charges on the books:** Qualcomm's own annual financial filings for fiscal 2026, covering the period through September 27, 2026, recorded $332 million in restructuring and related severance costs globally. That represents more than an eightfold jump from the $39 million booked the year before.

Put plainly, a company does not take a $332 million global restructuring charge just to clean up routine annual appraisals. Management is actively reshaping payroll costs and reweighting R&D budgets before the next fiscal year scales up.

## 4. Moving away from pure smartphone dependence

Qualcomm earned its market dominance by putting Snapdragon modems and processors inside hundreds of millions of Android smartphones every year. That foundation faces real pressure in 2026 as global phone upgrade cycles remain sluggish and top handset makers design more chips in-house.

To protect long-term growth, Qualcomm has been shifting engineering resources into four newer pillars:

- **Edge artificial intelligence:** Running local generative AI models directly on laptops, headsets, and industrial sensors.
- **Automotive electronics:** Digital cockpit displays, assisted-driving silicon, and connected-car telematics.
- **Connected IoT hardware:** Always-on wireless industrial devices and mixed-reality wearables.
- **Data center semiconductors:** Custom server acceleration and AI inference chips competing alongside traditional cloud suppliers.

Rebalancing toward data centers and automotive systems requires fewer legacy handset RF maintenance engineers and more specialized AI compiler, neural engine, and server architecture talent. India offices that spent a decade supporting smartphone chip generations now feel that portfolio shift directly.

## 5. How Qualcomm fits into the 2026 tech job market

Semiconductor and software workers across Bengaluru and Hyderabad are navigating one of the tightest job markets since 2023. Industry trackers show more than 80,000 technology positions were eliminated worldwide in the first quarter of 2026 alone, with total cuts for the year projected to pass 300,000 roles globally.

Across our broader tracking in the [2026 global tech layoffs report covering over 196,000 job cuts](/tech-layoffs-2026-intel-oracle-amazon-microsoft-meta-tcs/), Intel, Oracle, Amazon, Meta, and Indian services giant TCS all trimmed tens of thousands of jobs while pushing capital budgets toward AI infrastructure. Meanwhile, companies trying to avoid sudden termination headlines have turned to buyout windows, as seen in [Microsoft's 60,000-person Voluntary Exit Offer](/microsoft-veo-voluntary-exit-offer-layoffs-fact-check/).

For affected Indian engineers weighing relocation or overseas roles, immigration hurdles further narrow options following recent [US green card PERM processing shifts impacting Indian tech workers](/us-perm-suspension-microsoft-indian-it-vance-controversy/). As chipmakers keep chasing leaner margins, even senior hardware expertise offers less insulation against fast-moving budget resets than it once did.
