**Short answer:** On October 2, 2026, the Cockroach Janta Party held its announced protest at Shivaji Park in Mumbai after police denied permission. Thousands attended. A 75-year-old man died of a heart attack at the venue. In Delhi, police closed about a dozen metro stations, imposed an internet shutdown and detained at least 700 people, according to The Guardian. The CJP demands the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over the Special Intensive Revision. The Election Commission denies wrongdoing.

*Correction (2026-11-15): An earlier version said 11 metro stations were closed and attributed the Delhi details to a Hindu live blog whose headline rotates. It described the Gen Next Mumbai counter-protest as a defence of the Constitution, said a resident's complaint concerned silence-zone norms, and included Jaipur and Latur incidents this article could not verify. Those details are corrected or removed.*

## 1. What the CJP is demanding

The Cockroach Janta Party began as an online satirical movement. It grew into a street movement during the NEET paper-leak protests, and the education minister resigned in July 2026, according to The Guardian.

Its October 2 demands were specific. The Hindu reported that the CJP wanted Gyanesh Kumar to resign as Chief Election Commissioner, criminal charges filed against him, the SIR roll frozen, and the process of appointing the CEC opened to civil society.

The trigger was an Indian Express investigation. It reported that Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi recorded objections at least 14 times over 10 months to decisions related to the SIR, including changes to Form 6 and centralised access to the electoral roll database.

The Election Commission rejected the suggestion that those objections showed a breakdown. It said individual views are part of deliberation in a multi-member body, and that final decisions on the SIR were taken unanimously, Hindustan Times reported.

The Guardian reported that the SIR has resulted in tens of millions of people being deleted from the electoral register, and that large numbers are challenging their removal. The CJP calls this suppression. The commission calls the process lawful. That dispute is political and unresolved, and this article does not settle it.

## 2. Mumbai: permission denied, protest held anyway

Mumbai Police denied the CJP permission for Shivaji Park in a letter dated September 27. The Indian Express reported that police cited a 2013 Bombay High Court order restricting the ground to sports, the absence of BMC permission, the area's silence-zone status, and the risk of noise, traffic congestion and delayed access to nearby hospitals.

The CJP had also been denied permission at Azad Maidan, according to The Times of India. Dipke responded by announcing a "Jail Bharo Andolan" for Gandhi Jayanti.

The protest went ahead at Shivaji Park on October 2. The Guardian reported that thousands gathered in the afternoon amid a heavy police presence. Dipke had vowed to proceed regardless of the denial.

"We are fighting against those in power," Dipke said at a press conference, according to The Guardian. "We all remember Mahatma Gandhi, who fought against the Britishers. Did the British ever permit him to agitate?"

Film and music personalities joined the crowd. Naseeruddin Shah and Vishal Dadlani attended, and Shabana Azmi backed the protest, The Indian Express reported. The Economic Times and Free Press Journal also listed Azmi among those present.

Azmi made her case in an Instagram video a day before the protest. "Your vote is your right," she said, according to The Indian Express. "But what happens if your vote doesn't count? What happens when your name suddenly disappears from the voters list?"

No official crowd count was published. "Thousands" is the figure used by The Guardian and The Indian Express, and it should be read as an estimate rather than a tally.

## 3. The death at Shivaji Park

A 75-year-old man died after suffering a heart attack at the venue.

He was Gopalakrishna Subbaiya Pillai, a resident of Dosti West County in Thane, according to The Indian Express. He arrived at Shivaji Park around 4:30 p.m. to attend the protest. Around 5 p.m., near Gate No. 6, he complained of chest pain. A friend and police personnel came to his aid, and a doctor at the venue gave preliminary treatment. He was taken to Sushrusha Hospital in Shivaji Park and declared dead there.

Police said doctors attributed the death to a heart attack. An accidental death report was registered, and the body was handed to the family after they raised no objection.

The death became a political flashpoint. The New Indian Express reported that Shivaji Park residents demanded action against the CJP organisers. Resident Santosh Pavaskar said locals had submitted a representation two days before the protest asking authorities not to grant permission. He demanded a case against Dipke and his associates.

Advocate Siddhi Deolekar, a Shivaji Park resident of 10 years, filed a complaint at Shivaji Park police station. She told Hindustan Times that the CJP went ahead on the roads despite the denial and the High Court order. "I am not against protests," she said. "Everyone has the right to protest, but there are designated places for protests."

A complaint is an allegation, not a finding of guilt. The New Indian Express reported that police were examining the circumstances and the residents' allegations. No public report reviewed for this article found the CJP criminally responsible for Pillai's death.

## 4. Counter-protest and political reaction

A group called Gen Next Mumbai held a counter-protest near Mantralaya, the state secretariat. Free Press Journal reported that around 150 young men and women gathered near the Dr B.R. Ambedkar statue and raised slogans against Dipke, the CJP and Arvind Kejriwal. The report described the group as associated with right-wing organisations. The demonstration lasted about half an hour.

Maharashtra Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis rejected the CJP's allegations. "The entire matter has been exposed," he said, according to Mid-Day. "Following the press conference held by the three Election Commissioners, their fake narrative has been busted."

There was also a scuffle in Dadar as CJP supporters returned from Shivaji Park. Mid-Day reported that an argument broke out between activists of a political party and some protesters. Police intervened and dispersed those involved. The report did not name the party.

## 5. Delhi: metro closures, internet shutdown and detentions

Delhi became the harder-edged side of the story.

The Guardian reported that thousands of police were deployed to Jantar Mantar, about a dozen metro stations in the surrounding area were closed, and an internet shutdown was imposed. Hundreds of people who reached the protest site were forced into police buses.

The Guardian put the detentions at "at least 700 people, including civil society leaders and opposition politicians." Hindustan Times, reporting earlier in the day, said more than 500 had reportedly been detained, including AAP leaders Atishi and Sanjay Singh. The two figures are not necessarily contradictory: the count rose as the day went on.

Opposition parties said they would start their own nationwide movement demanding Kumar's resignation. Opposition lawmakers planned to march to the Election Commission office in Delhi the following week, The Guardian reported.

Protests also took place in Bengaluru, where the All India Students Association demanded Kumar's resignation, according to The Guardian. The Indian Express reported that Dipke planned to take the campaign to Goa, Bengaluru and Kolkata before a sit-in at Jantar Mantar.

## 6. What is verified and what is not

The core sequence is well documented. Police denied permission. The CJP proceeded. Thousands gathered in Mumbai. Pillai died of a heart attack at the venue. A resident filed a complaint. A counter-protest took place. Delhi saw metro closures, an internet shutdown and mass detentions.

Several details remain uncertain. There is no official crowd count for Mumbai. The number of closed metro stations is reported as "about a dozen," not a precise figure. The detention count ranges from more than 500 to at least 700 depending on when it was recorded.

The central allegation, that the SIR is a targeted voter suppression tactic, is contested. The CJP points to mass deletions and the reported internal objections. The Election Commission says the process was lawful and its final decisions were unanimous. Neither claim is settled by the protest itself.

What the record does show is that the October 2 protest became a test of how the state handles dissent over the electoral roll. Mumbai allowed the gathering to proceed and policed it heavily. Delhi closed transport, cut internet access and detained people in large numbers. Both responses are now part of the record the courts and voters will weigh.

**Q: Who was the man who died at the Mumbai protest?**
