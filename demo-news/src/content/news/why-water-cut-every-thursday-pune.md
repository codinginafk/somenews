**Short answer:** Pune Municipal Corporation will shut down citywide tap water every Thursday starting October 15, 2026. The move follows a Maharashtra state mandate ordering a minimum 10% water cut. The goal is to stretch storage in the Khadakwasla reservoir system until August 31, 2027, following weak monsoon rains.

## 1. What the shutdown announcement says

Starting Thursday, October 15, 2026, the Pune Municipal Corporation (PMC) will completely suspend water supply across Pune every Thursday. The weekly one-day closure applies across all municipal zones.

The civic body has issued specific instructions for residents and commercial establishments:

- Store sufficient water on Wednesday evening to cover domestic needs for Thursday.
- Inspect and repair private pipeline connections, leaking taps, and toilet flush tanks immediately.
- Stop using potable drinking water for private or public swimming pools, vehicle cleaning, and garden maintenance.
- Commercial kitchens, hotels, hostels, and colleges must curb water use in cleaning and laundry operations.

The PMC has also ordered the complete closure of vehicle washing centres across the city until the 2027 monsoon. Construction sites are barred from consuming drinking water and must switch to treated greywater.

## 2. The reservoir data behind the decision

The water cut is an advance conservation measure rather than an emergency pipe failure.

Pune relies on the Khadakwasla reservoir network for municipal supply. According to figures released by municipal authorities on October 12 and 13, 2026, storage across the system sits far below normal levels. In the Khadakwasla dam group, live storage stands between 26.22 and 38.78 thousand million cubic feet (TMC). At the same point in October 2025, storage was near 95% capacity.

The Maharashtra Water Resources Department issued a Government Resolution on October 6, 2026. The order directs urban local bodies to micro-plan dam usage and reduce municipal consumption by at least 10%. Cutting supply on one day out of seven achieves an automatic 14.3% theoretical reduction, satisfying the 10% state target with a buffer.

As Additional Municipal Commissioner Pavneet Kaur explained, the reduction aims to prevent dry taps in the peak summer of 2027. Monsoon precipitation dropped because of the El Niño weather pattern, which forecasters project will influence weather into mid-2027.

## 3. The broader Maharashtra drought picture

Pune is not acting alone. The state government declared 265 of Maharashtra's 358 talukas (nearly 74%) drought-affected in September 2026.

Monsoon data across Maharashtra shows the scale of the shortfall:

- The state recorded an overall 18.8% rainfall deficit during the 2026 monsoon.
- Six districts suffered extreme deficits between 40% and 50%.
- In Marathwada, major irrigation projects hold only 58.34% of capacity, compared with 97.30% in October 2025.
- Medium dams in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar hold just 21.12% of capacity, down from 76.23% in 2025.
- In Beed district, five out of eight reservoirs have run completely dry.
- In Dharashiv, seven out of nine irrigation projects hold zero usable water.

In response, Nagpur district banned drilling new borewells and deepening existing ones under Section 51 of the Disaster Management Act, 2005. Marathwada district officials ordered industries to recycle 30% of their intake and restricted agricultural water releases.

## 4. Practical advisory for Pune households

Households across Pune need to adjust their routines for the weekly cycle:

1. **Plan ahead on Wednesday:** Fill overhead tanks and clean storage vessels before Wednesday night. Low pressure can occur in tail-end areas during supply resumption on Friday morning.
2. **Reuse household wastewater:** Water from reverse osmosis purifiers and vegetable rinsing should be diverted to toilet flushing or plants.
3. **Report municipal leaks:** Leaks in public distribution mains should be reported immediately to the PMC water helpline to prevent treated water loss.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Will water supply resume normally on Friday morning?**
A: Yes, water supply resumes on Friday. However, residents in high-elevation or tail-end distribution zones may experience lower pressure for several hours as trunk mains refill.

**Q: Are hospitals and emergency services exempt from the Thursday shutdown?**
A: Critical facilities such as government hospitals receive priority tanker supply, but institutions have been ordered to maintain 48 hours of on-site buffer storage.

**Q: How long will the Thursday water cuts continue in Pune?**
A: The PMC stated the once-a-week suspension will remain in place until reservoir reserves are replenished by the next monsoon in 2027.
