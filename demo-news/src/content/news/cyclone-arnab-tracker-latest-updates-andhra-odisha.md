**Short answer:** Cyclone Arnab is tracking across the Bay of Bengal toward an expected landfall between coastal Andhra Pradesh and Yanam in Puducherry on Tuesday, October 27, 2026. While the eye aims for Andhra territory, state administrations across eastern India have launched broad protective measures. Odisha has enforced high-wind protocols for gusts up to 80 kmph, Andhra Pradesh has deployed 15 National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) teams alongside 2,613 water tankers, and meteorologists in Uttar Pradesh have posted yellow alerts for seven districts.

## 1. Andhra Pradesh landfall corridor and emergency mobilizations

The India Meteorological Department pinpointed the projected landfall sector along the central coast between Andhra Pradesh and the enclave of Yanam. 

State authorities in Amaravati initiated immediate precautionary restrictions across six vulnerable coastal districts: Kakinada, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar Konaseema, East Godavari, West Godavari, Krishna, and Anakapalli. 

To prevent loss of life and secure vulnerable settlements:

- Disaster teams on the ground: The state deployed 15 NDRF teams across designated high-risk pockets. Two teams moved directly into Yanam, three teams took positions at the NDRF district headquarters in Krishna, and three additional units assembled at the Regional Response Centre in Visakhapatnam.
- Public movement restrictions: District collectors ordered the closure of all educational institutions across the coastal corridor. Public access to beaches was blocked, and traffic on arterial coastal roadways was suspended from Tuesday afternoon onward.
- Essential utility protection: The municipal administration stationed 2,613 emergency water tankers in local depots to maintain drinking water access if pipeline grids suffer power outages or seawater infiltration.

Officials cited previous coastal storms, emphasizing that early road closures minimize stranded travelers during sudden squall lines.

## 2. Odisha issues zero casualty order and scenic site bans

Even with the cyclone center steered toward Andhra Pradesh, the storm outer wind field extends north across southern and coastal Odisha.

Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi stated that southern districts will experience intense precipitation and surface winds reaching 80 kmph. Disaster Management Minister Suresh Pujari announced that district collectors must adhere strictly to a zero casualty mandate, moving residents out of thatch dwellings and mud-walled homes before gale conditions arrive.

Odisha authorities enacted specific regional prohibitions:

- Banning all commercial marine fishing and pulling small vessels into protected harbors.
- Closing scenic spots, including hill trails, waterfalls, lake viewpoints, and river reservoirs, across six vulnerable districts to prevent tourist accidents during flash downpours.
- Preparing power repair squads and cyclone shelters stocked with dry rations and emergency medical supplies.

The administration highlighted that wide tropical systems pull deep moisture inland, creating flash-flood hazards well outside the direct landfall point.

## 3. Upstream impact: Uttar Pradesh yellow alerts for farmers

The weather system reach extends beyond the immediate Bay of Bengal coastline. Atmospheric models indicate unseasonal rain, thunderstorms, and brisk winds moving into parts of northern India between October 28 and October 31.

The IMD issued a formal yellow advisory for seven districts in eastern Uttar Pradesh: Sonbhadra, Mirzapur, Chandauli, Varanasi, Bhadohi, Ghazipur, and Ballia. Forecasts anticipate sustained winds between 30 and 40 kmph alongside sudden electrical storms.

The Uttar Pradesh Relief Commissioner Office instructed district teams to assist farming communities during the current paddy harvest season. Agricultural departments urged growers to secure harvested crops in covered yards to prevent rot from unexpected moisture.

State rescue agencies and medical centers have been placed on standby across eastern Uttar Pradesh to address power line disruptions or localized waterlogging.
