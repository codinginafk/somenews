**Short answer:** Senior Congress leader and Lok Sabha Leader of Opposition Rahul Gandhi was acquitted by an Uttar Pradesh special MP-MLA court on October 26, 2026. Additional Chief Judicial Magistrate Shubham Verma ruled that Gandhi's 2018 election campaign remarks regarding Union Home Minister Amit Shah did not constitute criminal defamation under Indian law. Gandhi appeared in person before the Sultanpur court to receive the verdict, closing a six-year legal dispute brought by a local Bharatiya Janata Party official.

## 1. What the complaint alleged

The dispute originated during the May 2018 campaign for the Karnataka Legislative Assembly elections. 

Addressing a party press conference and campaign rally in Bengaluru, Rahul Gandhi leveled sharp criticism against the top leadership of the Bharatiya Janata Party. Amit Shah was serving as national president of the party at the time. Gandhi referenced the 2005 Sohrabuddin Sheikh encounter case, remarking: "People in India forget that the president of BJP is a murder accused. That's the truth. The party that talks about honesty, decency, has a person who has been accused of murder as president."

Three months later, on August 4, 2018, local BJP leader Vijay Mishra filed a private criminal complaint in the Sultanpur district court. Mishra, a resident of Hanumanganj under Kotwali Dehat police station limits and former chairman of the Sultanpur District Cooperative Bank, argued that Gandhi's statements harmed the public reputation of the party president and caused personal mental distress to party workers.

The Sultanpur court entertained the private complaint and issued summons under Section 500 of the Indian Penal Code, which penalizes criminal defamation with up to two years imprisonment. The litigation stretched across six years of summons, revisions, and procedural hearings. Similar private defamation suits have increasingly become a regular feature in district courts against political commentators and politicians, as detailed in our analysis of [defamation warrants and court summons against public commentators](/dhruv-rathee-london-protest-vote-chori-defamation-warrant/).

## 2. What the court ruled

On October 26, 2026, Rahul Gandhi appeared in person before Additional Chief Judicial Magistrate Shubham Verma at the Sultanpur MP-MLA court complex.

Magistrate Verma delivered the final judgment acquitting the Congress leader on all counts. The court held that the statements made during the 2018 campaign rally failed to meet the essential statutory ingredients required to establish criminal defamation under Section 499 of the Indian Penal Code.

The verdict rested on three core legal considerations:

- **Absence of criminal intent in campaign rhetoric:** The court observed that critical statements delivered in the context of electoral debates do not automatically demonstrate the criminal malice required for a defamation conviction. Political debate allows broad latitude for criticism of party leadership.
- **Reference to public judicial history:** The defense submitted that Gandhi's statements referred to past judicial proceedings. Amit Shah had been named in a Central Bureau of Investigation charge sheet in 2010 before being discharged by a special Mumbai CBI court in December 2014, which found the accusations politically motivated. The magistrate accepted that discussing past public allegations without malicious fabrication does not equate to criminal defamation.
- **Strict standards on complainant locus standi:** The complaint was filed by an individual local party worker rather than by the person targeted in the speech. Under criminal procedure, a third party claiming hurt sentiments on behalf of a political organization faces strict evidentiary requirements to prove personal legal injury.

Following the pronouncement, Gandhi left the court premises while party supporters celebrated outside the complex, welcoming the formal termination of the case.

## 3. What's missing / the legal footnote

Criminal defamation under Section 499 and Section 500 of the Indian Penal Code has faced sustained scrutiny from civil liberties advocates. While civil defamation seeks financial compensation for proven reputational loss, criminal defamation introduces the threat of jail time.

The statute itself incorporates ten express exceptions designed to shield public discussion:

- **Exception 1 (Public good truth):** An imputation regarding any person is not defamation if it is true and made for the public good.
- **Exception 2 (Public conduct of public servants):** Opinions expressed in good faith concerning the conduct of public officials in the discharge of their public functions are protected.
- **Exception 3 (Conduct of persons touching public questions):** Good faith commentary on the character and actions of individuals involved in public affairs is safeguarded.

Courts have repeatedly emphasized that criminal law must not serve as an instrument to penalize partisan political rhetoric. As explored in our reporting on [judicial standards in speech and liberty disputes](/umar-khalid-human-rights-award-delhi-riots-uapa-facts/), higher courts routinely warn that political disputes belong on the campaign trail rather than in the criminal docket. 

The Sultanpur ruling provides relief for Gandhi, who had previously faced disqualification from the Lok Sabha in 2023 following a separate criminal defamation conviction in Surat, Gujarat, before the Supreme Court stayed that conviction. The decision affirms that historical references made during election campaigns do not easily cross the bar into criminal guilt.
