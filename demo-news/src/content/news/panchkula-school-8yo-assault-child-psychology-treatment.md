**Short answer:** The call for jail is not how this works. The Panchkula Police registered the case against an eight-year-old boy under the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 and the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, which puts the decision with a Juvenile Justice Board rather than a criminal court. That board granted him bail. An eight-year-old cannot be tried as an adult or sent to an adult jail. The outrage is aimed at the wrong target, because the accountability in this case runs the other way: police have registered separate cases against the school principal, the class in-charge and the security guard, and the Haryana government has suspended the principal and two teachers.

## 1. What the record establishes

The Indian Express, quoting the Deputy Commissioner of Police for Panchkula, Aditi Singh, reported on November 1 that a kindergarten student was left battling for life after an eight-year-old boy allegedly attacked her inside an empty classroom at a government school in Haryana's Panchkula, hitting her with a brick and a flower pot, banging her head against a desk and stomping on her face and body.

The girl sustained facial fractures, including a nasal fracture, and was undergoing treatment at the Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research in Chandigarh. The Hindu Business Line's sister report adds that she also suffered a minor fracture on her head. The same Indian Express report records that she regained consciousness on Saturday, the third day of her hospitalisation, and that her condition had improved.

The Statesman identifies the school as Sanskriti Model Senior Secondary School in Sector 20, and reports, from the father's bail application, that the boy was eight years and eight months old and in Class 3. India Today describes the victim as a kindergarten pupil with special needs.

The surveillance footage is the reason the case moved. The Indian Express reports that it shows the Class 3 student grabbing the girl by her hair and repeatedly banging her head against a desk, then kicking her and hitting her with a brick multiple times, continuing after she lost consciousness, and in a second clip returning to the classroom some time later and assaulting her again. The Hindustan Times notes that it could not independently verify the authenticity of the video.

One detail is worth separating from the rest, because it is the boy's own words as reported by the family. The girl did not go missing. According to the Indian Express and the Hindustan Times, the auto-rickshaw driver who came to collect her could not find her and told her father, who rushed to the school. The boy met him and said: "Uncle, I will take you upstairs. She is there." Her father found her unconscious in a pool of blood. The uncle said she had been lying there for more than two hours.

## 2. What the record establishes about the school

This is where the failures were, and they are documented rather than alleged.

The uncle's account, reported by the Indian Express and the Hindustan Times, is that the girl went up to her classroom at around 3.15 pm and that the assault probably took place between 4.15 pm and 4.30 pm. The boy told her "Tere papa aa rahe hain school", meaning your father is coming to school, and took her to another classroom. The uncle alleged that the boy hit her seven to eight times with a brick, banged her head against a bench and struck her with an aluminium dustbin, then left and returned about half an hour later.

On supervision, the uncle said: "The teachers told us that the principal's duty hours ended at 3 pm. The CCTV cameras were monitored from the principal's room. The footage shows the boy taking her from their classroom to another room. Had someone been present there, this incident would not have happened."

Police found prima facie lapses on the part of the school administration, Aditi Singh said, and registered an FIR under relevant sections of the Juvenile Justice Act and the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita. The Statesman reports that separate FIRs were registered against the school principal, the class in-charge and the security guard.

The state acted on both counts. The Indian Express reports that the Haryana government suspended the principal and two Junior Basic Training teachers, with the principal's suspension issued on Sunday under Rule 5 of the Haryana Civil Services (Punishment and Appeal) Rules, 2016, by Principal Secretary (School Education) Vijay Singh Dahiya. The government also constituted a five-member committee to probe the incident, ordered a comprehensive safety audit of schools across the state, and said it would bear the girl's entire treatment cost. Education Minister Mahipal Dhanda said no one found responsible for negligence would be spared.

The Haryana State Commission for Protection of Child Rights took suo motu cognisance, said the child was out of danger, and sought a detailed report from the Panchkula District Education Officer on the circumstances leading to the assault, the supervision arrangements and the action taken by the school. The Statesman reports that the Haryana Human Rights Commission also took cognisance of the matter.

## 3. What the law provides for an eight-year-old

The claim that the boy faces jail is wrong in the most basic way, and the reporting makes the mechanism clear.

India Today, citing police, says the boy, being a juvenile, was sent for psychological evaluation in accordance with the Juvenile Justice Act. The Statesman reports that the Juvenile Justice Board in Panchkula granted him bail, days after the incident, and that the board also directed that videos of the assault circulating on social media be removed.

The boy's father had approached the board on November 4 seeking bail. The Statesman reports that his application said the child was eight years and eight months old, had no previous criminal record, and promised counselling, behavioural guidance and rehabilitation. The family told the board it would take responsibility for the child's upbringing, education and medical care, and argued that custodial interrogation was unnecessary and that keeping the child in custody would serve no useful purpose.

The framework is the point. A child in this age group is not tried as an adult, and the Juvenile Justice Act keeps children out of police lockup and adult jails. The board's job is to assess what happened and what the child needs. The Statesman reports that the Haryana Education Department formed a committee to investigate the incident and examine possible lapses.

The result is the thing the outrage skipped. The complaint that drew the loudest response online was that he would go to prison. The documented outcome is that he was granted bail, sent for psychological evaluation, and had the videos of the attack ordered removed, while the adults who were on duty that afternoon were the ones booked.

## 4. What happened to the girl

PGIMER Chandigarh treated her free of cost. The Indian Express quotes the institute's director, Prof Vivek Lal, saying the entire treatment is being provided free of cost, that a private room was provided for her care and privacy, and that the institute would extend every possible assistance to the family. The Haryana government separately said it would bear her entire treatment cost.

India Today reported on November 4 that she had been discharged from hospital and had returned home. The same report records that a social worker, Anil Thapar, stepped forward and pledged to bear the entire cost of her education for as long as she continues her studies.

## 5. What the record does not settle

Three things remain open, and it is worth being clear about them.

The footage is the foundation of the case and no outlet has independently authenticated it. The Hindustan Times says so explicitly.

The clinical picture is not public. The boy was sent for psychological evaluation, and the reporting does not set out what the evaluators found, or any assessment of the child's conduct or state. Any account of his psychology, including accounts that describe him as a psychopath or as lacking remorse, is not on the record and is not in this article.

And the investigation is open. The police case against the school administration, the separate cases against the principal, class in-charge and security guard, and the state's five-member committee are all pending. The Statesman notes the authorities are still examining possible negligence.

## 6. The verdict: false, with context

The claim fails. An eight-year-old in India cannot be sent to jail, cannot be held in police lockup and cannot be tried as an adult. The Juvenile Justice Board granted him bail within days, ordered his psychological evaluation, and ordered the viral videos taken down.

The context is where the real failure sits, and it is the failure the outrage kept missing. This was a six-year-old kindergarten pupil with special needs, locked in an empty classroom for more than two hours, in a building where the principal's duty hours had ended and the cameras were watched from the principal's own room. The adults responsible for that room have been booked and suspended. The child who was in the room was granted bail.

That inversion is the story, and it is the one worth checking whenever a crowd reaches for a punishment that the law will not deliver. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Can the boy be sent to jail?**
A: No. He is a juvenile, the case is registered under the Juvenile Justice Act and the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, and the Juvenile Justice Board, not a criminal court, decides it. The Statesman reports the board granted bail.

**Q: What has the boy been ordered to do?**
A: He was sent for psychological evaluation in accordance with the Juvenile Justice Act, according to police as reported by India Today. His father's bail application promised counselling, behavioural guidance and rehabilitation, with the family taking responsibility for his upbringing, education and medical care.

**Q: Will anyone face charges for the girl's treatment or the failure to supervise?**
A: The state government bears the treatment cost and PGIMER is treating her free of charge. Police have registered separate cases against the school principal, the class in-charge and the security guard, and the principal and two teachers have been suspended while a five-member state committee investigates.

**Q: Is the boy treated as a violent child who understands what he did?**
A: That question is exactly what the psychological evaluation is for, and no findings have been published. Nothing in the record establishes his mental state, and this article makes no claim about it.
