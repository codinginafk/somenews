**Short answer:** Kim Jong Un did not die. The claim came from a single X account with a track record of false claims, it carried no evidence, and North Korea's state media answered it with photographs. Yonhap reported on October 20, 2026 that the Korean Central News Agency had published images of Kim touring the 2026 National Garment and Footwear Exhibition in Pyongyang with his daughter Ju-ae and his wife Ri Sol-ju. South Korean diplomatic and security officials said they had heard nothing to support the claims. The 9,000 Chinese troops were never verified by anyone, including the people who posted the story.

*Correction (2026-11-15): This article previously cited the Yonhap News Agency and New York Post homepages rather than the reports themselves. It now cites five specific articles. Four claims have been removed because the reporting does not support them: that Kim had appeared at a museum completion ceremony on October 11, that independent analysts had confirmed no protocol shifts inside Pyongyang, that the 1994 and 2011 leadership deaths were announced in a particular way, and that satellite imagery showed a standard border posture along the Yalu and Tumen rivers. The date on which state media released the photographs is given as reported by Yonhap and the Korean Central News Agency, which is later than the date carried by the New York Post's version.*

## 1. What the posts claimed

The story started with Wanjun Xie, a US-based Chinese dissident who identifies himself as chairman of the Chinese Democratic Party, an organisation banned by Chinese authorities. Yonhap reported that the claims were traced to a post on X by Xie, who had previously circulated other unverified claims, including speculation about Chinese President Xi Jinping's death and the alleged use of a body double.

In a post on Monday, according to Yonhap and The Times of India, Xie claimed that Kim had suffered a severe brain haemorrhage, lost consciousness and been transferred to Peking Union Medical College Hospital in Beijing because of North Korea's poor medical facilities. He claimed Kim had died, that his body had been secretly returned to Pyongyang and that the announcement was being withheld.

The Times of India records the wider version of the claim: that a team of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular medical experts had been dispatched to North Korea, that Kim had been moved to Beijing for emergency surgery, and that Kim Yo Jong had taken control of the country rather than Kim's teenage daughter, Kim Ju-ae. Xie also claimed China had sent 9,000 troops to Pyongyang.

The Economic Times reports that Xie's post alleged the troop deployment and that the claim was never independently verified. Yonhap's own summary is blunt: no evidence was provided to substantiate the claims.

## 2. What state media published

The answer came from Pyongyang rather than Seoul, and it came in pictures.

Yonhap reported on October 20 that the Korean Central News Agency had said Kim toured the 2026 National Garment and Footwear Exhibition the previous day, October 19, and expressed satisfaction at what he called the rapid and substantial development of light industry. According to the state media photographs, Kim was accompanied by his daughter Ju-ae and his wife, Ri Sol-ju.

The scale of the exhibition gives the visit a plausible reason to be filmed. Yonhap reports that more than 70,700 items of clothing of over 30 types and more than 170,000 pairs of shoes of over 60 kinds were on display, produced at light industry factories across the country under Kim's regional development policy. Kim was quoted by KCNA calling the show a demonstration of the Party's policy on eliminating differences between central and local areas and between urban and rural areas.

The New York Post published its own report on October 19, describing the images as apparent proof of life. It reported that Kim bends forward to inspect a tiny pink shoe held by a young woman in a pale pink double-breasted dress, stands with his legs apart and arms outstretched as he appears to address an official, and reaches toward the sleeve of a rust-coloured women's coat on a mannequin while aides trail behind him.

Two limits are worth stating. The New York Post reported that the photographs have not been independently authenticated and that it remains unclear exactly when they were taken. And Yonhap noted that North Korean state media did not directly address the speculation at all. It answered the rumours by publishing the visit, not by denying anything.

## 3. What South Korea's assessment said

Yonhap reports that South Korean diplomatic and security officials said they had heard nothing to support the claims.

The most specific assessment came from the National Intelligence Service. The Times of India, citing the New York Post, reports that the NIS told lawmakers on September 30 that Kim weighed approximately 140 kilograms, or 309 pounds, and faced an elevated risk of cardiovascular disease because of severe obesity. The agency reportedly detected no significant signs of ill health, pointing to Kim's ability to run up stairs during an inspection of a naval destroyer.

That is the detail that answers the rumours directly. A leader who cannot climb a flight of stairs on a warship is not the subject of a claim about a haemorrhage, a coma and a secret airlift.

## 4. Why this pattern repeats

The pattern is old, and the reporting documents it.

The Times of India notes the latest rumours echo a false alarm in 2020, when Ji Seong-ho, a North Korean defector who later became a South Korean lawmaker, claimed he was 99% certain that Kim had died following cardiovascular surgery. Kim subsequently appeared in public, and Ji apologised for making the erroneous claim.

The structural reason is in the sources. Yonhap notes that Xie circulated the claims while Kim had not made a public appearance to deny them, and The Times of India records that the speculation gathered pace because Kim had not yet appeared publicly. A closed media environment turns a gap in the public record into a blank cheque.

The people who filled that gap were also the people with the least reason to be believed, and the reporting says so. Yonhap notes Xie's own history of circulating unverified claims about Xi Jinping and body doubles. The Economic Times reports that the claims of death, Chinese troop deployment and succession crisis remain unverified. The New York Post says it sought comment from the governments of North Korea and China, and from Peking Union Medical College Hospital.

## 5. The verdict: false, with context

The claim is false on every limb. Kim did not die of a cerebral haemorrhage. His body was not flown back to Pyongyang. Kim Yo Jong did not seize the regime. And the 9,000 Chinese troops exist only in a post that carried no evidence and that the people who posted it have a documented record of getting it wrong.

The context is that the answer came from the same state media that produced the rumours' subject, and nobody outside Pyongyang has authenticated a photograph. What is documented is narrower and still decisive: the Korean Central News Agency published images of Kim at a public exhibition on October 19, 2026, and South Korean intelligence told lawmakers he showed no significant signs of ill health as of September 30.

A death claim that needs a photograph to survive is not a death claim.

The same pattern runs through the stories we have checked, from [unverified biological facility claims](/russia-siberia-plague-lab-fact-check/) to [celebrity death hoaxes](/nana-patekar-death-updates/): the gap is real, and the filling is not.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Did China send 9,000 troops to Pyongyang?**
A: No. The Economic Times reports that Xie's post alleged the deployment and that it was never independently verified. No other source in this record confirms it.

**Q: Did Kim Yo Jong take control of North Korea?**
A: There is no evidence of a transfer of power. State media reported Kim opening the exhibition and speaking on party policy, and the reporting describes Kim Yo Jong's claim as part of the rumour, not as a reported event.

**Q: Has Kim Jong Un been seen since the rumours?**
A: The Korean Central News Agency published photographs of him at the National Garment and Footwear Exhibition on October 19, 2026. The New York Post reported that the photographs have not been independently authenticated, and that it is unclear exactly when they were taken.

**Q: Is his health the real question?**
A: It is a fair question, and the answer from the National Intelligence Service as reported by the New York Post and the Times of India is that as of September 30 he weighed about 140 kilograms, faced elevated cardiovascular risk because of severe obesity, and showed no significant signs of ill health.
