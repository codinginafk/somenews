Same map, new colors, each month. One side shouts safest. The other shouts collapse. Both skip method.

Three sources feed these maps. They count different things.

Numbeo Safety Index: crowd votes on a site. Sample = who visits Numbeo. In 2025, top cities logged 2,000 to 8,000 votes. Small towns logged under 200. Young, urban, English-speaking users tilt the pot. A city can jump 20 spots after one viral thread. I watched it happen to a Gulf city in June. Votes tripled in a week. Rank jumped. Streets did not change.

Gallup Law and Order: phone and face interviews, n near 1,000 per country. Asks: "Do you feel safe walking alone at night?" Plus theft and assault reports. This gauges fear plus recall. Gulf states and Nordics score high here. Latin cities score low. Fear tracks lighting, policing, and news diets as much as events.

UNODC: police files sent by states. Counts homicide well. Counts assault and robbery poorly across borders. Definitions split. Filing rates split more. The handbook itself warns against league tables. Makers quote the file and skip the warning.

My 30-second check before I share any map:

- Who voted or answered? Look for n and mode. Under 500, stop.
- What was the exact ask? "Safe at night" is not "low crime."
- Which year? 2021 mixes lockdown streets with normal ones.
- City or country? A calm capital can mask rough ports in one mean.

I keep a folder of these maps. Nine of the last ten that hit my feed used crowd votes but wrote "crime data." That word swap is the tell. Votes are views. Files are files. Surveys are surveys. Name the lane or drop the post.
