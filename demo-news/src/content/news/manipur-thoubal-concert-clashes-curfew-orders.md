**Short answer:** The confrontation at Khangabok in Thoubal district was far from a routine public argument. On Sunday night, October 18, 2026, stone-pelting at an open-air concert escalated into inter-community violence, gunfire, and arson. Twenty-six-year-old Laishram Rakesh was fatally shot in the head, multiple residents sustained injuries from stones, and houses and vehicles were burned, forcing district authorities to clamp emergency night curfews.

## 1. Stone-pelting at the CYSA playground

The violence began on Sunday evening at the CYSA playground in Khangabok village, situated roughly 25 kilometers south of Imphal under the jurisdiction of the Thoubal police station. A musical concert was underway when an unidentified group hurled stones into the crowded venue.

The sudden attack triggered immediate retaliation between attendees from Khangabok and groups from neighboring Sangaiyumpham. Within minutes, the dispute widened into running street battles across village boundaries.

Police dispatches confirmed that stone-throwing quickly gave way to arson. Unidentified armed individuals fired multiple rounds into the crowds, creating widespread panic among concertgoers trying to flee the enclosed ground.

## 2. Gunfire, casualties, and night arson

During the exchange of fire, 26-year-old Laishram Rakesh was struck in the head by a bullet. Bystanders rushed him to a nearby medical facility, where doctors pronounced him dead on arrival.

Several other individuals were admitted to local hospitals suffering from lacerations and head injuries caused by flying stones. As the crowds dispersed into residential lanes, miscreants set fire to multiple houses and parked vehicles in both Khangabok and Sangaiyumpham before security units could establish cordons.

The violence coincided with a separate attack in Imphal, where an IED detonated at Kakwa Lamdaibung Lampak near the Singjamei police station, wounding three CRPF personnel on patrol.

## 3. Emergency prohibitory orders and security buildup

Fearing that retaliatory attacks would spread into adjoining valley areas, the Thoubal District Magistrate issued an emergency order on Sunday night prohibiting public movement from 11:00 PM onward.

The magistrate declared an "imminent danger to human life and property because of the unlawful activities of some antisocial elements," directing all residents of Khangabok and Sangaiyumpham to remain indoors under strict home confinement.

State police, backed by central paramilitary battalions, initiated joint area domination operations on Monday morning.
