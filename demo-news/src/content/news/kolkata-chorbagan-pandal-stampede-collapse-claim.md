**Short answer:** On the evening of October 17, 2026, a microphone announcement at the Chorbagan Sarbojanin Durga Puja pandal in north Kolkata warned that the structure was in bad condition and would collapse. Police have described the announcement as misleading. No official account reviewed for this article describes the pandal giving way, and the structure was still standing when a judicial panel ordered it preserved for examination. The panic sent devotees toward a narrow exit gate, where 19 people died and 25 were injured.

*Correction (2026-11-15): An earlier version said forensic teams had confirmed there was no structural collapse, attributed a hospital patient breakdown to the West Bengal Clinical Establishment Regulatory Commission, and said a sitting High Court judge would lead an inquiry into safety at all major Puja sites. The record supports a narrower account: police called the collapse warning misleading, the forensic report focused on ventilation and cooling, the Chief Minister gave the hospital update, and the state set up an SIT headed by a sitting Calcutta High Court judge plus a parallel judicial probe panel led by retired Justice Anindita Roy Saraswati. The earlier version also described an "emergency corridor" that no source mentions.*

## 1. What happened at Chorbagan

The Chorbagan Sarbojanin pandal sits in Sarada Park in north Kolkata, under Girish Park police station. On Saturday, October 17, 2026, it was hosting its Durga Puja crowd on Maha Saptami. The incident occurred around 7:40 pm at the exit gate, according to Kolkata Police accounts cited by Hindustan Times.

Heavy rain had pushed more people into the pandal while those already inside wanted to leave. The Times of India reported that the gates and the approach road were narrow. As panic spread, visitors tried to escape through the entrance.

Then came the announcement. A video being examined by Kolkata Police captures a voice saying: "The condition of the pandal is very bad. It will collapse. Please leave." Police said the announcement may have triggered the panic. The Times of India reported that officers were trying to identify who made it and what prompted it.

## 2. Did the pandal collapse?

The viral claim is that the pandal suffered a structural collapse during the Saptami celebrations. That framing spread quickly on the night of October 17, when reports described a section of the temporary structure giving way under crowd pressure.

The later record does not support it. Kolkata Police described the announcement as misleading, and the case registered at Girish Park police station is one of culpable homicide not amounting to murder, based on alleged negligence in crowd management. No police statement or court filing reviewed for this article describes the bamboo and cloth structure collapsing.

The physical evidence points the other way. When authorities discussed immersing the idols, the judicial probe panel said the pandal itself would remain standing and its structure must be preserved for examination. The panel, led by retired Justice Anindita Roy Saraswati, inspected the site and flagged the narrow entry and exit points and dim lighting as factors in the confusion.

That does not mean the pandal was safe. It means the danger came from the crowd, the layout and the warning, not from a collapsing roof. The same pattern of narrow routes and unmanaged crowds has appeared in other [festival safety reviews](/devaragattu-banni-festival-stick-fight-kurnool-clashes/) and in [public transit restrictions during large gatherings](/cjp-delhi-mega-protest-crackdown/).

## 3. What police say went wrong

Kolkata Police have pointed to gross mismanagement by the organizers. A senior officer said there was no clear plan to manage the large number of visitors and that everything was handled verbally. Announcements about entry, exit and crowd movement were inadequate, leaving visitors confused.

Police also found that the number of police personnel and private security guards at the venue was inadequate. Local residents alleged that officers were mainly stationed near the VIP entrance. The Times of India reported that investigators were examining whether the deployment worsened the situation.

The whistle claim came from the assistant commissioner of police at the spot. Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari, relaying that assessment, said an announcement by a private security agency and the blowing of whistles may have triggered the incident.

## 4. Arrests, FIR and investigation

Police registered a suo motu case under Sections 105, 110 and 3(5) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita. Seven people were arrested, including committee secretary Raj Kumar Sewda alias Raja, 48, and vice-president Santanu Dubey, 55. The others were Biplab Mukherjee alias Joy, 48, Kaushik Saha, 53, Pintu De, Suman Chakraborty and Rajesh Pal. Fourteen more people were detained.

The state government constituted a Special Investigation Team headed by a sitting judge of the Calcutta High Court. A parallel judicial probe panel is led by retired Justice Anindita Roy Saraswati. On October 23, police arrested Kushal Majumdar alias Babu for allegedly making the misleading announcement. The main announcer, Sanjib Raha alias Sentu, had been arrested earlier from Nadia district.

Kolkata Police Commissioner Ajay Kumar Nand imposed prohibitory orders under Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita on roads adjoining the pandal until October 20. The orders, in force from October 18 to 20, barred assemblies of five or more people and the carrying of lathis or dangerous weapons.

## 5. The forensic report and the aftermath

The forensic report focused on the pandal's air-conditioning and ventilation, not on a structural collapse. Kolkata Police sources told ETV Bharat and Lokmat that 24 AC units had been installed, with a combined capacity that the two reports put at 134 to 139 tonnes. Experts said that was inadequate for the crowd and the pandal's dimensions. The report said overcrowding exceeded the pandal's capacity and that poor airflow likely worsened the panic.

The human toll is not in dispute. Nineteen people died and 25 were injured. Chief Minister Adhikari said 24 of the injured were out of danger and one was critical. The state announced ₹25 lakh and a Group D government job for the family of each deceased victim, and ₹5 lakh for each injured person, according to Oneindia.

The pandal remained standing. Its idols were immersed, but the structure was preserved for the investigation. That is the opposite of the collapse story that circulated on the night of October 17.

## 6. What remains unresolved

The investigation has not produced a final finding on the exact sequence inside the pandal or on who authorized the announcement. Two announcers are in custody, and police have said they are still establishing what prompted the warning. The judicial panel and the SIT are running parallel inquiries.

The rain was real, and police acknowledge it contributed to the crowd surge. The organizers have pointed to the weather. The forensic report adds a separate finding about ventilation and cooling. Those explanations are not mutually exclusive.

What the record does not support is the claim that the pandal collapsed. The deaths followed a false warning and a crush at a narrow exit.
