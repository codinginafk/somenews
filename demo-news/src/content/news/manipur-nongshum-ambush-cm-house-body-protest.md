**Short answer:** On Monday morning, October 19, 2026, suspected militants ambushed a party of 14 Meitei villagers collecting firewood in the Sunurok hills near Nongshum, killing three men and wounding three others. The attack triggered intense civil fury in Imphal, where grieving residents carried two of the corpses directly to the entrance gate of Chief Minister Yumnam Khemchand Singh's official bungalow in Babupara before security forces dispersed them with tear gas.

## 1. Ambush in the Sunurok hills

The victims, all residents of Nongshum village located in Imphal East under the Sagolmang police station, entered the forest bordering Kangpokpi district around 7:00 AM on Monday to cut and transport firewood.

Around 9:40 AM, unidentified armed militants positioned on elevated ridges opened fire without warning. ADGP (Law and Order) Lhari Dorjee Lhatoo reported that two villagers, 35-year-old Arubam Ronil and 51-year-old Lairenlakpam Inaobi, were killed instantly on the spot.

A third worker, 32-year-old Kshetrimayum Sushil, was evacuated with severe bullet trauma but died after reaching a private hospital in Imphal. Three additional firewood collectors (N Suraj, 19; Meisnam Somendro, 53; and K Bosan, 45) sustained gunshot injuries and remain hospitalized. The ambush represents the first lethal attack targeting members of Manipur's majority community since January 21, 2026.

## 2. Tear gas at the Chief Minister's gate

Following the shooting, enraged local residents brought the bodies of Ronil and Inaobi into the heart of the state capital. The demonstration reached the official residence of Chief Minister Yumnam Khemchand Singh in Babupara, where protesters laid the two corpses on the street outside the main gate.

Demonstrators refused to withdraw until police units fired several rounds of tear gas shells into the crowd. Security personnel recovered the bodies and transported them to the mortuary at the Jawaharlal Nehru Institute of Medical Sciences.

Police cordoned off all approach roads to the Chief Minister's bungalow with razor wire and armored vehicles. In the surrounding valley, residents blocked traffic at Khurai Lamlong, while women vendors staged demonstrations at the historic Khwairamband Ema Keithel market, forcing commercial shutdowns across Imphal.

## 3. The political debate over SoO accords

The ambush sparked sharp political reactions. Former Chief Minister N Biren Singh, who resigned prior to the imposition of President's Rule in 2025, visited the hospital and openly attacked claims of stabilization: "Everyone has been told that peace has returned. As a Manipuri and as a Meitei, we cannot keep counting the dead. Continued killings have made people feel orphaned." Congress Legislative Party leader Keisham Meghachandra demanded that Chief Minister Singh step down.

Civil organizations also mobilized. The Coordinating Committee on Manipur Integrity demanded an immediate cancellation of the tripartite Suspension of Operations pact with armed Kuki groups. The Meitei Heritage Society questioned why a nearby security post was shifted in June despite warnings of vulnerability.

Conversely, the Khamenlok Area Core Committee, representing local Kuki-Zo residents, condemned the senseless loss of life while cautioning against communal stereotyping: "There is a grave possibility that vested interests might attempt to falsely pin the blame on the Kuki-Zo community to instigate further communal polarization." ## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Who were the victims of the Nongshum forest ambush?**
A: Three villagers from Nongshum were killed: Arubam Ronil (35), Lairenlakpam Inaobi (51), and Kshetrimayum Sushil (32). Three other collectors (N Suraj, Meisnam Somendro, and K Bosan) were hospitalized with gunshot wounds.

**Q: Why were bodies brought to the Chief Minister's residence?**
A: Grieving relatives and local residents carried two corpses to the main gate of Chief Minister Yumnam Khemchand Singh's bungalow in Babupara to demand accountability and protest recurring attacks, leading police to deploy tear gas.

**Q: What is the Suspension of Operations (SoO) pact mentioned by civil groups?**
A: The SoO is a tripartite ceasefire agreement between the Union government, the Manipur state government, and various Kuki-Zo insurgent groups. Civil bodies in the valley have repeatedly demanded its formal revocation following border ambushes.
