**Short answer:** The attack is real and the heroism is documented. On September 30, 2026, an Omani first officer attacked Captain Smit Machchhar with a cockpit crash axe aboard flydubai Flight FZ1073, sending the Boeing 737 MAX 8 into a steep dive before passengers subdued him and two off-duty pilots landed in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia. All 180 people aboard survived. The "World War III" framing is speculation: no official described the incident that way, and investigators have characterized it as an attempted terrorist attack and suicide crash plot.

*Correction (2026-11-14): An earlier version said the flight carried 180 passengers, that it was at 14,000 feet, and that unnamed "military experts" warned the crash could have sparked a regional war involving nuclear powers. The aircraft carried 172 passengers and 8 crew, descended from about 34,000 feet to 17,000 feet, and no official source in the record supports the World War III claim. Those assertions have been removed.*

## 1. What happened in the cockpit

Flight FZ1073 left Dubai at 7:05 a.m. local time on September 30, bound for Ben Gurion Airport near Tel Aviv. About two hours into the scheduled three-and-a-half-hour flight, near the Saudi-Jordan border, the first officer, Hamam al-Hammami, attacked the captain.

According to passenger accounts and official statements, al-Hammami struck Machchhar on the back of the head with the cockpit crash axe. Machchhar had turned forward after al-Hammami said he wanted to pray. The first officer then put the aircraft into a steep descent.

The jet fell from roughly 34,000 feet to about 17,000 feet in under a minute and reached an estimated 600 miles per hour, according to flight telemetry cited by Wikipedia. The speed exceeded the aircraft's design limit and tore away most of the rudder. The transponder sent code 7700, a general emergency, then 7500, which signals hijacking.

Despite deep cuts to his head and hands, Machchhar released the cockpit door from inside. Passengers rushed in. A passenger seated in the front row, Tzvika Manes, and others pulled al-Hammami off the captain. A dentist on board treated Machchhar. Two flydubai pilots who were deadheading in the cabin, Rowan Tooth of South Africa and Henson Steven of Fiji, took the controls and landed at Prince Sultan bin Abdulaziz Airport in Tabuk at 10:45 a.m. local time.

## 2. What the investigation confirmed

The aircraft was a Boeing 737 MAX 8 registered as A6-FKF. There were 172 passengers, 150 of them Israeli citizens, and 8 crew. No one died. Machchhar was initially in serious condition and later reported stable.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said one pilot had stabbed the other before "apparently trying to crash" the plane. Israeli and UAE officials called the incident an attempted terrorist attack. UAE prosecutors later said al-Hammami had planned to crash the aircraft into a terminal at Ben Gurion Airport in a suicide attack, and investigators believed the plan was inspired by the September 11 attacks.

Al-Hammami, a 29-year-old Omani national, had joined flydubai in February 2026. He had previously been a co-pilot trainee at Oman Air, which suspended him from flying in 2024 after extremist material was found in his possession, assigned him an administrative role and enrolled him in a deradicalization program. Oman Air did not share its concerns broadly. Flydubai ran a criminal background check when it hired him but did not contact Omani authorities to verify his employment history, The Wall Street Journal reported.

The Israeli Air Force scrambled fighter jets after the hijacking code and could not reach the aircraft for about 30 minutes. Israeli protocol permits a rogue aircraft approaching Israel to be shot down under certain conditions with the prime minister's approval. The jets were recalled after the plane turned back toward Saudi Arabia.

## 3. Why "World War III" is a stretch

The claim that the attack could have triggered World War III rests on a chain of hypotheticals: a crash with mass Israeli casualties, immediate retaliation against a state or group, and escalation involving major powers. Some of those ingredients were present. The flight carried 150 Israeli citizens. The attacker was an Omani national. US President Donald Trump threatened to hit Iran "very hard" if it was behind the thwarted attack, based on what he said he was hearing, without providing details.

But no official in the record said the incident nearly caused a world war. Netanyahu called it an attempted terrorist attack and said it was too early to establish who was behind it. The UAE investigated whether it was linked to terrorism. The concrete escalation that followed was diplomatic and security-focused: Israel suspended flydubai flights for six days, the UAE agreed to Israeli security demands, and several airlines adjusted routes.

The "World War III" phrase appears to have spread through social media commentary, not official briefings. Treating it as fact turns a documented security failure into a movie plot. The more defensible conclusion is that the attack was a serious insider threat with real geopolitical context, and that the outcome could have been far worse without the captain and passengers.

## 4. What the incident exposed

The case raised a question aviation security had largely avoided: what happens when the attacker is the pilot. Reinforced cockpit doors were designed to keep hijackers out. They also made it harder for the crew to intervene when the threat came from inside the flight deck.

Aviation security experts quoted in the record compared the incident with the 2015 deliberate crash of Germanwings Flight 9525 and said crew screening and monitoring need more attention. The prior warnings about al-Hammami at Oman Air suggest the gap was not a lack of information but a failure to share it across airlines and regulators.

For India, the case also became a domestic security matter. The National Investigation Agency registered a case under the Unlawful Activities Prevention Act and the Anti-Hijacking Act to examine whether a larger conspiracy existed. The UAE awarded its First Class Order of Zayed II to the flydubai pilots and cabin crew on October 27.

The Middle East remains a region where aviation incidents can escalate quickly, as seen in [the aftermath of attacks on Saudi infrastructure](/saudi-airport-attack-indian-casualties-fact-check/) and in [the pressure on hospitals during the Gaza war](/nasser-hospital-gaza-strike-surgical-ward-facts/). That context explains why officials treated FZ1073 as a security emergency. It does not turn a single cockpit attack into a world war.
