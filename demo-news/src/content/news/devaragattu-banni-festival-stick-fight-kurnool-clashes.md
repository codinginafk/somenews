**Short answer:** Claims that administrative measures prevented serious injuries at the 2026 Banni festival in Andhra Pradesh are disproven by the casualty reports. One devotee died and the injured count ranges from more than 60 to more than 100, depending on the outlet. The casualties occurred during the annual stick fight at Devaragattu in Kurnool district. About 1,300 police personnel were deployed, along with CCTV monitoring and medical camps, and thousands of villagers still clashed with bamboo sticks to capture temple idols.

*Correction (2026-11-15): An earlier version gave a single injury figure of at least 70 and a single age of 32 for the deceased, and included details about Section 144 orders, iron-tipped bamboo poles and routine surgical bed reservations that the cited reporting does not support. Reports differ on the casualty count and the victim's age. This version states the range and attributes each figure.*

## 1. What official directives and local claims promised

Ahead of the annual Dasara celebrations, Kurnool district authorities launched public awareness campaigns across Holagunda mandal. Officials urged residents from surrounding rural settlements to abandon the traditional stick-fighting ritual known as Karrala Samaram.

Police outlined heavy security arrangements to prevent physical confrontations. The New Indian Express reported that the district police deployed nearly 1,300 personnel. The Times of India reported that authorities also installed CCTV cameras and ran awareness campaigns urging participants to refrain from violence. Hindustan Times reported that medical camps were set up at the venue.

Local civic commentary claimed that tighter administrative oversight and preventive peace committees would transform the event into a peaceful observance. Organizers expressed confidence that ceremonial gatherings would proceed without the violent confrontations that marked past editions.

## 2. What the casualty reports confirm

The ritual began late on Wednesday, October 21, 2026, and continued past midnight. It opened with the Kalyanotsavam of Sri Mala Malleswara Swamy, followed by the procession of the temple deities down the hill. At that point, rival village contingents clashed to gain possession of the festival idols. Local tradition divides the villages into opposing factions reenacting a mythical victory, believing that securing the idols brings agricultural prosperity for the coming year.

Nearly 3,000 villagers armed with long bamboo sticks and flaming torches fought across the rocky paths, according to The Hindu and The New Indian Express. A crowd of nearly 300,000 devotees from Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Telangana gathered along the hill slopes to watch, The Hindu reported.

One devotee died. Reports identify him as Pothuraju, also spelled Pothu Raju and Pothurai, a resident of Aluru village. His age is given as 28 by Hindustan Times and NewsMeter, 32 by The Times of India, and 35 by The Hindu and The New Indian Express. The reports agree that he died after being struck or collapsing during the clash. The New Indian Express said he suffered cardiac pain after being seriously injured.

The injured count is also reported differently. The Hindu and The New Indian Express said at least 70 people were hurt. The Times of India said more than 60. Hindustan Times and NewsMeter said more than 100. Eight people with serious injuries were shifted to Adoni Area Hospital, according to The Hindu and The New Indian Express. Others were taken to the government hospital in Aluru, according to NewsMeter and Hindustan Times. The New Indian Express reported that two people were in critical condition.

Hindustan Times reported that the fight continued for more than half an hour on the hill, which is about 800 feet high.

## 3. The enforcement dilemma: Tradition versus public safety

The recurring bloodshed at Devaragattu highlights the persistent challenge Indian administrative authorities face when policing deeply rooted ritual combat.

Despite security deployments and awareness drives held every year, local villagers view participation in the mock battle as a binding ancestral duty. The New Indian Express reported that villagers consider severe injuries leading to bleeding to be a good omen. NewsMeter reported that some devotees treat the shedding of blood during the ritual as auspicious. Neither group files cases against the other, The New Indian Express reported, even when the injuries are severe.

Police forces face severe constraints due to the sheer size of the crowd. Managing a surge of 300,000 pilgrims across steep, unpaved terrain limits the ability of security personnel to intervene once clashes ignite. Excessive force by police risks triggering mass stampedes down the hillside, forcing officers into a defensive posture focused on medical evacuation rather than physical confrontation. Similar crowd management and containment challenges have been documented in recent [festive crowd safety reviews](/kolkata-chorbagan-pandal-stampede-collapse-claim/) and our analysis of [public transit restrictions during large gatherings](/cjp-protest-crackdown-jantar-mantar-oct-10/).

## 4. What the numbers do and do not show

There is no single official casualty tally in the public reporting. The range runs from more than 60 injuries to more than 100, and the victim's age ranges from 28 to 35. That spread is a reason to state the figures carefully, not a reason to doubt that the violence was serious.

What the record does show is that the security measures did not prevent a death and dozens of injuries. The district deployed about 1,300 police personnel, installed cameras and set up medical camps. The ritual went ahead, the clashes occurred, and the injured were carried to hospitals in Aluru and Adoni.

The Times of India reported that local administration officials and police launched an investigation into the escalation. Medical teams continued to monitor patients in critical care. Those inquiries have not produced a public finding reviewed for this article.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Where does the Banni festival take place?**
A: The ritual occurs annually at the Sri Mala Malleswara Swamy Temple atop Devaragattu hill in Holagunda mandal of Kurnool district, Andhra Pradesh.

**Q: Why do villagers fight with sticks during the festival?**
A: Villagers believe that capturing the processional deities during the midnight descent brings good harvest and prosperity to their respective villages. Some also treat the bloodshed as auspicious.

**Q: How many people were killed and injured in 2026?**
A: One devotee died. Reports put the injured between more than 60 and more than 100, with The Hindu and The New Indian Express saying at least 70.

**Q: What security steps did police take before the event?**
A: Authorities deployed about 1,300 police personnel, installed monitoring cameras, ran awareness campaigns and set up medical camps. The turnout of nearly 300,000 devotees overwhelmed field enforcement across the dark hill slopes.
