**Short answer:** Claims that administrative measures prevented severe injuries at the 2026 Banni festival in Andhra Pradesh are disproven by official casualty registers. One devotee died and at least 70 others sustained serious injuries on Wednesday midnight, October 21, 2026. The casualties occurred during the annual stick fight at Devaragattu in Kurnool district. Despite heavy police deployment and surveillance cameras, thousands of villagers clashed with bamboo sticks to capture temple idols.

## 1. What official directives and local claims promised

Ahead of the annual Dasara celebrations, Kurnool district authorities launched public awareness campaigns across Holagunda mandal. Officials urged residents from surrounding rural settlements to abandon the traditional stick-fighting ritual known as Karrala Samaram.

Police statements outlined heavy security arrangements to prevent physical confrontations. Law enforcement deployed police pickets, installed surveillance cameras across the Devaragattu hillock, and set up medical outposts to monitor crowd movements. Revenue teams conducted peace committee meetings with village elders from Aluru, Neraniki, and surrounding hamlets.

Local civic commentary claimed that tighter administrative oversight and preventive peace committees would transform the event into a peaceful observance. Organizers expressed confidence that ceremonial gatherings would proceed without the violent confrontations that marked past editions.

## 2. What casualty logs and hospital records confirm

Hospital records and emergency incident logs reveal that the ritual degenerated into intense violence shortly after midnight.

Following the celestial wedding ceremony of Sri Mala Malleswara Swamy, priests began the midnight procession of the temple deities down the hill. At that moment, rival village contingents clashed violently to gain possession of the festival idols. Local tradition divides the villages into opposing factions reenacting a mythical victory, believing that securing the idols brings agricultural prosperity for the coming year.

Nearly 3,000 villagers armed with long bamboo sticks and flaming torches engaged in fierce combat across the rocky paths. A massive crowd of nearly 300,000 devotees from Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, and Telangana gathered along the hill slopes to watch the ritual.

One devotee, identified as Pothuraju, aged 32, a resident of Indira Nagar in Alur village, collapsed amid the fighting and died. Police confirmed that medical personnel transported at least 70 injured devotees to nearby government facilities. Initial field estimates from local rescue volunteers placed the total number receiving first aid above 100.

Emergency teams transferred eight critically injured participants with severe skull fractures and deep lacerations to Adoni Area Hospital. Other wounded devotees received urgent trauma care at the Alur Government Hospital and Kurnool district medical centers. Medical staff operated round the clock to treat head trauma, eye injuries, and bone fractures resulting from the heavy bamboo blows.

## 3. The enforcement dilemma: Tradition versus public safety

The recurring bloodshed at Devaragattu highlights the persistent challenge Indian administrative authorities face when policing deeply rooted ritual combat.

Despite Section 144 orders and awareness drives held every year, local villagers view participation in the mock battle as a binding ancestral duty. Devotees carry customized bamboo poles often tipped with iron rings, ignoring repeated police appeals to surrender weapons before climbing the hill.

Police forces face severe constraints due to the sheer size of the crowd. Managing a surge of 300,000 pilgrims across steep, unpaved terrain limits the ability of security personnel to intervene once clashes ignite. Excessive force by police risks triggering mass stampedes down the hillside, forcing officers into a defensive posture focused on medical evacuation rather than physical confrontation. Similar crowd management and containment challenges have been documented in recent [festive crowd safety reviews](/kolkata-chorbagan-pandal-stampede-collapse-claim/) and our analysis of [public transit restrictions during large gatherings](/cjp-delhi-mega-protest-crackdown/).

Local health officials noted that while fatalities during Karrala Samaram are irregular, severe traumatic injuries remain routine. Emergency wards in Adoni and Aluru routinely set aside dedicated surgical beds and blood supplies in advance of Dasara night.

Following the latest tragedy, district officials launched an inquiry into the security breaches. Medical teams continue monitoring several critically wounded patients in intensive care units.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Where does the Banni festival take place?**
A: The ritual occurs annually at the Sri Mala Malleswara Swamy Temple atop Devaragattu hill in Holagunda mandal of Kurnool district, Andhra Pradesh.

**Q: Why do villagers fight with sticks during the festival?**
A: Villagers believe that capturing the processional deities during the midnight descent brings good harvest and prosperity to their respective villages.

**Q: What security steps did police take before the event?**
A: Authorities deployed police personnel, installed monitoring cameras, and set up emergency medical camps. However, the massive turnout of 300,000 devotees overwhelmed field enforcement across the dark hill slopes.
