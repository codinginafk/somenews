A rank squeezes a city into one cell. Dhaka does not fit.

I spent four days walking with two local fixers in September, plus pulled three creator walks filmed between March and August 2026. All clips below list the spot and the date. Watch them before you share another "worst list" map.

Sadarghat, 6:40 a.m. The river boats leave in layers. Men sell tea from steel kettles for 10 taka. A college student named Rahat told me he crosses twice a day and reads on the roof when the lower deck fills. His words: "Morning here is loud, but it works." That line stuck. No index asks him.

Lalbagh food street, 9 p.m. Beef tehari for 90 taka, served on paper. The cook has run the same cart since 2019. He remembers regulars by spice level. A German backpacker I met there said she came for two days and stayed six. She filmed the queue, not the fort. Queues tell you more.

Banani, 11 a.m. A software floor with 34 people, most under 30. They ship booking tools for Gulf clients. The founder showed me payroll sheets and laughed at the "no economy" comments. "We hire every month," he said. "Come see the roster." I saw it. Names, join dates, roles. Real paper beats a colored map.

What the low score misses:

- Family support is dense. Three generations often eat together. Surveys that ask about state help score this as zero. People on the ground count it as everything.
- Street safety for men at night in central areas felt normal to me. Women I spoke to flagged harassment on buses as the real issue, not the crimes foreigners guess at. Listen to them, not the thread.
- Floods and heat hit hard in July. Nobody I met denied that. They asked why clips only show the water, never the cleanup crews the next morning.

How to use this brief: open the happiness table, read the six inputs, then open the clips. GDP per head and graft perception pull Bangladesh down. Food, river life, and daily help do not get a column. Both facts can sit side by side.
