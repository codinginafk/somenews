*Correction (2026-11-15): An earlier version of this article linked to the Ministry of External Affairs and The Hindu homepages, spelled Dammam as "Daman", and placed the Shaybah oil field strike a day before the airport strike. The MEA statement covers both strikes on October 21, and every source now points to the specific report or official statement.*

**Short answer:** The claim is wrong, and the official record is specific about why. An aerial strike on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam on the night of Wednesday, October 21, 2026 killed a Saudi citizen and an Indian national and injured eight people. An AFP tally of official figures put the death toll from strikes on Saudi airports at 22 for the month. That was not a series of isolated incidents. It was the deadliest run of attacks on Saudi soil since the Qatif and Dammam mosque bombings of May 2015.

## 1. What the Dammam strike actually was

Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation confirmed that two people died at King Fahd International Airport in Dammam, in the country's eastern oil city, on the night of October 21, and that eight others were injured. The Hindustan Times, reporting the GACA statement, records that the injured included nationals of India, Yemen and Bangladesh, along with a Saudi resident of Bangladeshi nationality.

The attack came from Yemen, claimed by the Houthis, and formed part of a coordinated strike on Saudi infrastructure. On the same day, a drone targeted an oil facility at Saudi Aramco's Shaybah field in the Eastern Province. The Saudi energy ministry said on Wednesday that falling debris from the interception of that drone killed two people and injured two more.

India's Ministry of External Affairs put out a statement covering both incidents. The Times of India reported it: one Indian national was killed and two others were injured in the attack on Dammam airport, and another Indian national was injured in the attack on the Shaybah oil field. The MEA said attacks on "civilian and critical infrastructure are completely unacceptable", called for them to stop, and said the Indian Embassy in Riyadh was in touch with Saudi authorities and would provide all possible assistance to affected Indians.

The embassy confirmed the death on Friday, according to Gulf News, and said it was assisting the family. It had posted that it was gathering information and reaching out to the affected families, with "thoughts and prayers" for the deceased and a wish for a speedy recovery for the injured.

## 2. The month total, and why it is not a rounding error

The Hindustan Times reports that according to an AFP tally based on official figures, attacks on Saudi airports had killed 22 people by October 22. Wikipedia's consolidated account of the same series gives 23 dead and more than 380 injured across three Saudi airports in October. A one-person difference between a running tally assembled by a wire service and a compiled chronology is what a live count looks like, and neither number is small.

The Indian casualty count is the sharpest rebuttal to the claim that foreign worker casualties are negligible. Three Indian nationals died in the October airport attacks: two men who died from their injuries in the days after the October 10 strike on terminals 3 and 4 at King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, and the national killed at Dammam on October 21.

The October 10 strike at Riyadh was the deadliest. Wikipedia records that twelve people were initially killed, including four Saudis, two Bangladeshi citizens and one each from the United States, Jordan, Palestine, Syria, Sudan and Egypt, with another 308 injured. The Bangladeshi Ministry of Foreign Affairs said the dead included a mufti and that 24 of the injured were Bangladeshi. Surveillance footage of the impact in the terminal hall, where pilgrims and travellers were queued at departure gates, showed a bright orange flash and an explosion that caved in sections of the ceiling.

## 3. The rest of the October timeline

The National's running timeline sets out the sequence. On October 6 and 7, a combined missile and drone attack killed one Sudanese national and injured eight civilians at King Khalid International Airport. Abha International Airport was also attacked, killing two women, from Algeria and Morocco, and wounding 28 others.

On October 8, two attacks targeted King Khalid International Airport, destroying a Saudia Airbus A321-200 and damaging two nearby aircraft including a Saudia A321neo. GACA said three Saudi citizens were killed, including the pilot, and dozens of people of different nationalities were wounded. Hundreds of flights were cancelled. Wikipedia records that Air India, Akasa Air with the exception of Dammam, Lufthansa and Pakistan International Airlines suspended services to Riyadh, and that operations returned to normal the following day.

On October 11, terminal 4 was struck again and a fire started in one of its passenger halls. A Pakistani resident died and eleven people were injured. Dammam airport was also attacked that day, with no casualty information reported at the time. On October 12, three ballistic missiles were fired, one towards King Khalid airport, and all three were intercepted.

The closure of King Khalid International Airport was then extended by a GACA notice until 21:00 GMT on October 17. Reuters, in a separate report on October 26, described Riyadh's airport as the kingdom's second-busiest after Jeddah and noted that the October 10 attack was the deadliest in the kingdom in more than a decade.

## 4. The diplomatic response, including from New Delhi

Saudi Arabia's coalition spokesperson Turki al-Maliki called the October 10 attack a "full-fledged war crime" and a "cowardly terrorist attack by the terrorist Houthi militia" that "resulted in deaths and injuries among civilian passengers and airport staff", and vowed to respond decisively in accordance with customary international humanitarian law. At the WPC Energy Congress in Riyadh on October 11, Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman Al Saud warned of a "serious response".

The United Nations weighed in on the same day. Secretary-General António Guterres strongly condemned the "lethal attack by the Houthis", said he was "deeply shocked", and said attacks on civilian infrastructure violated international law and "must cease immediately". UN special envoy for Yemen Hans Grundberg called the attacks on civilian airports in Saudi Arabia "a grave escalation" that "perpetuates cycles of violence".

The United States acted on its own citizens' risk. On Wednesday, October 21, the US ordered non-essential embassy staff and their families to leave Saudi Arabia. State Department spokesperson Tommy Pigott said in a social media post that "Iranian-supported Houthis have increasingly engaged in hostilities against Saudi Arabia, including attacks on civilian airports and other critical infrastructure resulting in civilian casualties and severe travel disruptions". Wikipedia records that travel cautions against Saudi Arabia were also issued by Canada, Japan, Poland, South Korea, New Zealand and the United Kingdom.

## 5. Why this escalated, and the context the claim leaves out

The October attacks did not begin in October. The Hindustan Times traces them to a years-long truce in Yemen's civil war collapsing in July, after which the Houthis announced a maritime blockade targeting Saudi Arabia and began attacking its ships, territory and oil facilities. In September they launched a major offensive and captured areas along the Red Sea coast that had been under government control, and the Saudi air force responded with repeated strikes on Houthi-controlled areas.

Wikipedia adds the specific trigger. On July 13, airstrikes struck Sanaa International Airport, which Human Rights Watch's analysis of satellite imagery showed had put three munitions on the airport's only operational runway. The stated purpose was to stop an Iranian aircraft landing, carrying passengers and dignitaries returning from the funeral of Iranian Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, who had been killed on the first day of the 2026 Iran war. The Houthis ended the UN-brokered truce initiated in 2022, imposed a blockade on Saudi Arabia and launched large-scale offensives.

Houthi official Mohammed al-Bukhaiti claimed responsibility for the October attacks and framed them as retaliation for Saudi strikes on civilian infrastructure in Yemen, arguing that airports, seaports, hospitals and markets in Yemen are civilian facilities and that the same rules must apply to their counterparts elsewhere. The group's military spokesman Yahya Saree warned airlines not to use Saudi airports or airspace, with the exception of Makkah and Madina.

## 6. The verdict

The claim that these strikes are isolated incidents with negligible foreign worker casualties is false on every part of it. Twenty-two people died in strikes on Saudi airports in October, more than 380 were injured, and the series is the deadliest against Saudi civilians since 2015. Three Indian nationals were among the dead. Airports closed, airlines suspended services to Riyadh, and governments including the United States and the United Kingdom issued travel cautions.

The context that belongs alongside the finding is the one that makes it a fact-check rather than a statistic. The Houthis state plainly that they are deliberately targeting civilian aviation hubs, and they have said so on the record. That is a deliberate policy aimed at a specific economic objective, and it is the reason airports rather than military bases are on the list. The human cost lands on the people who work on tarmacs and in terminals, including Indian nationals, and on the passengers queued at departure gates when a terminal roof caves in.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: How many people died in the Dammam airport attack?**
A: Two. Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation confirmed that a Saudi citizen and an Indian national were killed at King Fahd International Airport on October 21, 2026, and that eight people were injured.

**Q: How many people died in strikes on Saudi airports in October 2026?**
A: An AFP tally of official figures put it at 22 by October 22, and Wikipedia's consolidated account of the series gives 23 dead and more than 380 injured across three airports.

**Q: Were any Indian nationals killed?**
A: Yes, at least three. Two Indian men died from their injuries in the days after the October 10 strike on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, and one Indian national was killed at Dammam on October 21. A further Indian national was injured at the Shaybah oil field.

**Q: What did the Indian government say about the strikes?**
A: The Ministry of External Affairs called attacks on civilian and critical infrastructure "completely unacceptable", called for them to stop, and said the Indian Embassy in Riyadh was in touch with Saudi authorities to assist affected Indian nationals. The embassy had also issued safety guidance asking Indians to follow Saudi emergency alerts and move immediately to the nearest safe place inside a building.
