**Short answer:** While the university initially dismissed the massive student protests as a reaction to baseless rumours, the student body maintains they were revolting against a real incident that was improperly handled. Their timeline of events recently received preliminary backing from senior police officials, highlighting long-standing frustrations over campus safety.

## 1. What the viral post shows

For the students blocking the Delhi-Amritsar highway, the unrest was the culmination of a tragedy they believe the university tried to bury. According to protesting students, a female student was assaulted in the G-3 hostel roughly three days before the chaos erupted. Rather than launching a transparent investigation, they claim the university quietly sent the student home. When her roommate allegedly informed others that the student had subsequently died by suicide, the frustration transformed into an open campus revolt involving over 2,000 students.

The students argue that the administration's immediate reaction—denying the reports and calling them false and fabricated—was a symptom of a broader pattern of suppressing safety concerns rather than addressing them.

## 2. What the data actually says

Initially, local police like SSP Kapurthala Gaurav Toora seemed to back the university's stance, stating there were no prior complaints and attributing the outrage to an old, unrelated video. However, the students' narrative gained significant weight when DIG (Jalandhar Range) Naveen Singla announced that preliminary investigations supported the students' timeline. He confirmed that an incident had indeed occurred about three days earlier, leading the police to formally register an FIR based on recorded statements and invite student union representatives to participate in the probe.

Regarding the campus vandalism, student videos and accounts assert that the initial protests were completely peaceful. They claim the situation escalated when the university gates were opened, allowing non-students to enter and create a ruckus.

## 3. What's missing / the footnote

The core of the student protest extended far beyond a single incident. The unrest represented a breaking point over persistent demands for better hostel safety, including a major push for all-female staffing in the girls' hostels. To the student body, the administration is often slow to respond and unwilling to be transparent about ongoing campus safety issues.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Did the students cause the campus damage?**
A: Protesting students claim their demonstrations were peaceful until outsiders entered the campus through open gates and initiated the vandalism. Police are currently assessing the damage to determine legal responsibility.

**Q: Are the students' demands primarily about the reported assault?**
A: While the reported assault was the catalyst, students are actively pressing for broader institutional changes, including improved security measures and all-female staffing in women's hostels.
